Matched replacement contrasts for the healthy-versus-other binary endpoint. The exposure and non-exposure replacement policies remove identical donor groups, retain the same training-image and class budgets, and evaluate identical anchors (Fig. 1). (a) Class-balanced accuracy difference, exposure minus matched replacement control, on selected probes. Positive values describe increased apparent performance on anchors whose sources supplied training views under exposure; they are not a demonstrated improvement in deployment accuracy. (b) Difference in class-balanced correctness turnover on never-exposed sentinels. For each policy, turnover is the proportion, with endpoint-class balance, changing from correct to wrong or from wrong to correct relative to the shared zero-exposure fit; the plotted contrast is exposure turnover minus matched-control turnover. A near-zero turnover contrast does not mean either policy leaves sentinel predictions unchanged and does not establish equivalence. Markers are means of 30 split-level estimates. Each split estimate averages both complementary allocations and five allocation pairs. Bars span the 2.5 and 97.5 percentiles of those 30 estimates; because splits overlap, they are descriptive variation, not confidence intervals or 30 independent field experiments. Onion uses 481 conservative provenance components and 3076 supported augmented images; each split contains 97 probe anchors, 48 sentinel anchors and 240 training groups (480 images), with 48 probes selected in each arm. Potato uses 538 original leaf-ID groups and 2152 colour photographs; each split contains 108 probe anchors, 54 sentinel anchors and 268 training leaves (536 images), with 54 probes selected in each arm. The potato archive originates from a different collection project and is an additional retrospective task, not a newly collected field cohort. Original leaf IDs do not establish plant identities or unified pathological review. For this binary endpoint, class balance averages healthy and all other archive labels, independently of the multiclass weights. All three prespecified model settings are retained. Their model-wise contrasts and task heterogeneity are reported without selecting a winner or pooling the two tasks into a universal exposure effect.
